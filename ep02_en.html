Channel-Length Modulation, Output Resistance and Body Effect

Why does a MOSFET's current keep increasing in saturation? Explore channel-length modulation, output resistance, and body effect.

1Intro: a lab graph that slopes upward

Z

Zundamon

Metan, my lab report is in trouble! The current increased even in saturation!

M

Shikoku Metan

Let's review. In saturation, a MOSFET is a current source controlled by VGS.

Shikoku Metan: Last time, the saturation curves sloped upward slightly as VDS increased.

M

Shikoku Metan

That isn't a measurement error. Let's explore that slope, ro, and the body effect.

Z

Zundamon

Please! I want to explain the results in my report, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

The numbers are illustrative values, assuming roughly a 180 nm CMOS process.

M

Shikoku Metan

Last time's square law was ID = ½K·Vov², where K means μnCox·W/L.

Shikoku Metan: W is channel width, L is length. This model uses K = 2 mA/V².

2Measuring the slope

M

Shikoku Metan

Hold VGS at 1.2 V and raise VDS from 1.0 V to 1.5 V.

Z

Zundamon

ID rose from 0.704 mA to 0.736 mA. Just a little!

M

Shikoku Metan

Less than five percent, but that difference matters in an amplifier.

Z

Zundamon

An ideal current source wouldn't change its current when VDS changes.

M

Shikoku Metan

Right. This slope measures how imperfect it is as a current source.

3The cause: the pinch-off point moves

Z

ZundamonQuestion

But I thought the saturation curve was flat. Why does it slope?

M

Shikoku Metan

Back to the cross-section. In saturation, the channel ends near the drain.

M

Shikoku Metan

The pinch-off point stays at about Vov relative to the source.

Shikoku Metan: The rest of VDS appears across the gap between pinch-off and the drain.

Shikoku Metan: Raising VDS widens that gap, moving pinch-off toward the source.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

So the channel gets shorter?

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes, its effective length decreases slightly. That's channel-length modulation.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Why does a shorter channel mean more current?

M

Shikoku Metan

The channel voltage stays at Vov, while its length decreases.

Shikoku Metan: ID is proportional to W/L, so reducing L increases ID.

4The equation: ID ≈ ½KVov²(1 + λVDS)

M

Shikoku Metan

We model this simply by multiplying the square law by (1 + λVDS).

M

Shikoku Metan

λ is the channel-length modulation coefficient. Here, λ = 0.1 /V.

5Output resistance ro and the equivalent circuit

M

Shikoku Metan

At fixed VGS, ro is the inverse slope: ΔVDS divided by ΔID.

Shikoku Metan: Replace the saturated MOSFET with a current source in parallel with ro.

Shikoku Metan: The source gives ½KVov²; adding VDS/ro gives ID.

Z

Zundamon

Like the Norton equivalent circuit from circuit theory!

M

Shikoku Metan

Exactly. Infinite ro means an ideal source. Larger ro gets closer to ideal.

M

Shikoku Metan

Differentiate with respect to VDS: slope = λ·½KVov². Its inverse is ro.

Shikoku Metan: If λVDS ≪ 1, ID ≈ ½KVov², so ro≈1/(λID).

M

Shikoku Metan

At VGS=1.2 V, ½KVov² = 0.64 mA, giving ro≈16 kΩ.

Z

Zundamon

And 0.5 V divided by 0.032 mA gives about 16 kΩ. It matches!

Zundamon: But using the graph's ID of 0.72 mA gives a different answer?

M

Shikoku Metan

About 14 kΩ. It's off by roughly ten percent, but useful for hand estimates.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Does ro depend on the current?

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. At VGS=0.8 V, current is about a quarter, and ro is four times larger: 62 kΩ.

M

Shikoku Metan

Compare different λ values. Larger ro means less slope and a flatter curve.

Z

Zundamon

Small ro gives a steeper upward slope!

6Early voltage VA

M

Shikoku Metan

Keep λ at 0.1 and extend the saturation lines toward negative VDS.

Z

Zundamon

They all meet at VDS = −10 V!

M

Shikoku Metan

They meet exactly in this model. The voltage magnitude is VA=1/λ.

Shikoku Metan: Then ro≈VA/ID. A larger Early voltage means a better current source.

7Channel length L and λ

Z

ZundamonQuestion

How can we make ro larger?

M

Shikoku Metan

One way is to make the channel length L longer.

M

Shikoku Metan

The same pinch-off shift is a smaller fraction of a longer channel.

Shikoku Metan: So increasing L tends to reduce λ.

M

Shikoku Metan

Very short channels in advanced processes tend to have larger λ and smaller ro.

8Why ro matters: intrinsic gain gm·ro

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Why is small ro a problem?

M

Shikoku Metan

With an ideal current-source load, one amplifier stage has gain magnitude gm·ro.

Shikoku Metan: That's intrinsic gain. gm measures how ID changes with VGS; more next time.

M

Shikoku Metan

Ignoring λ, gm = K·Vov=1.6 mS. Times about 16 kΩ gives roughly 25.

M

Shikoku Metan

Including λ gives gm≈1.8 mS and an intrinsic gain of about 28.

Z

Zundamon

So small ro means small gm·ro and less amplification.

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. Short-channel devices often need circuit techniques to recover gain.

9Body effect

Z

Zundamon

Last time, we assumed the body was connected to the source.

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. But NMOS bodies in an IC are often connected together to ground.

M

Shikoku Metan

Stack transistors, and the upper source sits above ground.

M

Shikoku Metan

VSB is source voltage minus body voltage. Positive VSB raises Vth.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Why does Vth change if VGS stays the same?

M

Shikoku Metan

Positive VSB widens the depletion layer beneath the channel.

M

Shikoku Metan

More ion charge remains, so a larger gate voltage is needed to form the channel.

Shikoku Metan: This is called the body effect, or substrate-bias effect.

M

Shikoku Metan

Here's the formula. Vth rises with VSB, but the increase gradually slows.

Shikoku Metan: γ and 2φF are process constants: here 0.4 √V and 0.7 V.

M

Shikoku Metan

At VSB=0.5 V, Vth rises from 0.40 V to about 0.50 V.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

So the current falls even with the same VGS?

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. Holding VGS and VDS at 1.2 V, ID falls from 0.72 mA to about 0.54 mA.

Shikoku Metan: Include this effect when designing circuits whose sources aren't grounded.

10Summary

M

Shikoku Metan

Channel-length modulation moves pinch-off, making ID increase with VDS.

Shikoku Metan: ro is the inverse slope. Larger ro means a better source and higher intrinsic gain.

Z

Zundamon

And positive VSB raises Vth and reduces current!

Zundamon: That slope wasn't a measurement error! I'll calculate ro and fix my report!

M

Shikoku Metan
