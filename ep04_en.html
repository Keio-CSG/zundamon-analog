2Review: increasing RD

M

Shikoku Metan

Recall common-source gain: Av = −gm(RD∥ro).

Z

Zundamon

I thought a larger RD would give more gain.

M

Shikoku Metan

Make RD ten times larger, 50 kΩ. At the same current, the drop is about 9 V.

Z

Zundamon

But the supply is only 1.8 V! That's nowhere near enough!

M

Shikoku Metan

A larger resistor raises both signal resistance and DC voltage drop.

3Using a current source as the load

M

Shikoku Metan

We want fixed DC current with a high small-signal resistance.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

You mean a current source?

M

Shikoku Metan

Right. In episode three, a DC current source became a small-signal open circuit.

Shikoku Metan: An ideal current-source load gives Av = −gm·ro, the intrinsic gain.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

How do we build such a current source?

4Creating a current source with a diode connection

M

Shikoku Metan

A saturated MOSFET is a current source controlled by VGS.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

How do we find the VGS that gives the current we want?

M

Shikoku Metan

Connect the gate to the drain. This is called a diode connection.

Shikoku Metan: Feed in a reference current, Iref, from outside.

M

Shikoku Metan

The MOSFET then establishes the VGS that carries exactly Iref.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Is it still in saturation with gate and drain connected?

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. VDS=VGS, so the saturation condition holds whenever VGS exceeds Vth.

Shikoku Metan: For Iref=0.1 mA, VGS is about 0.70 V.

5The current mirror

M

Shikoku Metan

Add a second MOSFET and connect their gates together.

M

Shikoku Metan

Same size, same VGS: the right-hand device carries the same current.

Shikoku Metan: It copies like a mirror, so it's a current mirror. Call the output current Iout.

Shikoku Metan: Double the right-hand W/L and Iout doubles to 0.2 mA.

M

Shikoku Metan

ID scales with W/L, assuming matched parameters and equal VDS.

6Mirror error from ro and the saturation condition

Z

ZundamonQuestion

So it's exactly the same current whatever circuit we connect on the right?

M

Shikoku Metan

Not necessarily. First, return both devices to the same W/L.

M

Shikoku Metan

Left VDS is about 0.70 V. Right VDS depends on the connected circuit.

Shikoku Metan: At right VDS=1.5 V, Iout≈0.107 mA, about seven percent higher.

M

Shikoku Metan

The slope is 1/ro from episode two. Exact matching needs equal VDS.

M

Shikoku Metan

Too little VDS is also a problem: at 0.2 V, Iout≈0.080 mA, outside saturation.

Shikoku Metan: Keep the output device saturated: here, VDS must be about 0.3 V or more.

7A closer look at PMOS

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Can we just put that mirror in place of RD?

M

Shikoku Metan

The load sits above the output. With NMOS, its source and VGS would move.

M

Shikoku Metan

Use the PMOS from episode one. Its source can connect to VDD.

M

Shikoku Metan

Its source arrow points inward, with a circle at the gate and source on top.

Shikoku Metan: It turns on with the gate below the source, so use VSG.

M

Shikoku Metan

When VSG exceeds |Vth|, current flows from source to drain, top to bottom.

Shikoku Metan: Use the NMOS equations with VSG, VSD, and |Vth|; positive current runs S to D.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Is the current magnitude the same as NMOS too?

M

Shikoku Metan

Holes move less easily. This model halves K for the same W/L.

M

Shikoku Metan

A PMOS mirror works too, like the NMOS mirror flipped upside down.

8The common-source amplifier with an active load

M

Shikoku Metan

Use that PMOS mirror as the load. A transistor load is called an active load.

M

Shikoku Metan

M1 amplifies, M2 is the load, and diode-connected M3 receives Iref.

Shikoku Metan: VDD=1.8 V, Iref=0.1 mA. M2 and M3 match; each body is tied to its source.

M

Shikoku Metan

VOUT is where M1 and M2 carry equal current. Overlay their curves.

Z

Zundamon

The intersection is the operating point!

M

Shikoku Metan

For VOUT=0.9 V, VIN is about 0.70 V and current about 0.1 mA.

Shikoku Metan: Sweep VIN to get this transfer curve. Much steeper than episode three!

9Small-signal equivalent circuit and Av

M

Shikoku Metan

Let's derive the gain by transforming the small-signal circuit.

Shikoku Metan: Both M1 and M2 must be saturated: output roughly between 0.3 V and 1.4 V.

M

Shikoku Metan

Step one: short the DC voltages and open the reference current source.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Doesn't opening Iref leave M2's gate floating?

M

Shikoku Metan

M3 connects it to signal ground through a resistance of about 1/gm3.

M

Shikoku Metan

No signal enters there, so its gate signal is zero and vsg2=0.

Shikoku Metan: Step two: gm2·vsg2=0, leaving only ro2 in M2's model.

Z

Zundamon

So the current-source load looks like resistance ro2 for small signals!

M

Shikoku Metan

M1 is the same as before: a gm1·vin source in parallel with ro1.

M

Shikoku Metan

Step three: ro1 and ro2 both connect output to ground, so they're parallel.

Shikoku Metan: RD has become ro2: Av = −gm1(ro1∥ro2).

10Intrinsic gain and a numerical example

Z

ZundamonQuestion

How does that compare with gm·ro for an ideal source load?

M

Shikoku Metan

If ro1=ro2, their parallel resistance halves: Av = −gm·ro/2.

M

Shikoku Metan

The load is a real transistor too. Its finite ro reduces the gain.

M

Shikoku Metan

gm1 is about 0.65 mA/V; ro1 and ro2 are both about 108 kΩ.

Shikoku Metan: In parallel, about 54 kΩ gives Av ≈ −35, half the intrinsic gain of 70.

Z

Zundamon

It matches the slope! That's about nine times episode three's gain magnitude!

M

Shikoku Metan

This bias differs too: Vov≈0.3 V increases intrinsic gain to about 70.

11Setting the operating point

M

Shikoku Metan

But beware: a 10 mV VIN change moves VOUT by about 0.35 V.

M

Shikoku Metan

Both current curves are flat, so their intersection moves a long way.

Shikoku Metan: Practical circuits use negative feedback to stabilize the operating point.
