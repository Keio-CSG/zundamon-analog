The Differential Pair

Amplifying a microphone signal also amplifies its noise. Explore how a differential pair amplifies the difference between two inputs and rejects common-mode interference.

2Why differential signals?

M

Shikoku Metan

In real circuits, supply and ground voltages wobble a little with noise.

M

Shikoku Metan

In episode four, a 10 mV input shift moved the output by about 0.35 V.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

What if the source's ground and the amplifier's ground differ by 10 mV, nanoda?

M

Shikoku Metan

Same as a 10 mV offset at the input. So use two inputs and amplify only the difference.

M

Shikoku Metan

If the wobble hits both inputs equally, taking the difference cancels it.

Z

Zundamon

Even if both shift by the same amount, the difference stays the same, nanoda!

3The differential-pair circuit

M

Shikoku Metan

Place two identical NMOS, M1 and M2, and tie their sources together. Call that point P.

M

Shikoku Metan

Pull a constant current ISS out of P. We call this the tail current source.

M

Shikoku Metan

Each drain gets RD. The inputs are VIN1 and VIN2, the outputs VOUT1 and VOUT2.

M

Shikoku Metan

VDD=1.8 V, ISS=0.2 mA, RD=5 kΩ. Both inputs sit at 1.3 V DC.

M

Shikoku Metan

The body is grounded. P is about 0.49 V, so we include the body effect.

M

Shikoku Metan

With equal inputs it's symmetric. Each side carries half: 0.1 mA.

4Large-signal behavior

Z

ZundamonQuestion

What happens if the two inputs differ, nanoda?

M

Shikoku Metan

At P, ID1+ID2=ISS. The total is always 0.2 mA.

M

Shikoku Metan

Raise VIN1 and M1 carries more current, so M2 carries less.

M

Shikoku Metan

Plot the current difference ΔID against the input difference ΔVin like this.

M

Shikoku Metan

Near zero it's nearly a straight line, slope about gm, both sides adding up.

M

Shikoku Metan

With a large difference, M2 is nearly off. Almost all current flows through M1.

M

Shikoku Metan

With the square law, one side turns off near √2·Vov, about 0.43 V.

M

Shikoku Metan

Vov is about 0.30 V when balanced. In this model, some current remains at 0.43 V.

Z

Zundamon

So with a big difference, it's a switch steering current left or right, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

Conversely, move both inputs together and P just follows. Each side still carries half.

5Differential and common-mode signals

M

Shikoku Metan

Now small signals. Split inputs vin1 and vin2 into two components.

M

Shikoku Metan

Differential signal: vid=vin1−vin2. Common-mode signal: vic = (vin1+vin2)/2.

Shikoku Metan: Conversely, vin1=vic+vid/2 and vin2=vic−vid/2.

Z

Zundamon

It's a linear circuit, so superposition lets us solve them separately, nanoda!

6Differential half-circuit and gain

M

Shikoku Metan

Differential first. M1's gate gets +vid/2 and M2's gate gets −vid/2.

M

Shikoku Metan

The inputs are opposite, so their pushes on P cancel. P's small signal is zero.

M

Shikoku Metan

For small signals, P acts like ground. We call this a virtual ground.

M

Shikoku Metan

The left half is exactly episode three's common source. That's the half circuit.

M

Shikoku Metan

The source doesn't move, so there's no body-effect term either.

M

Shikoku Metan

vout1 = −gm(RD∥ro)·vid/2. vout2 has the opposite sign, so the difference doubles.

M

Shikoku Metan

Differential-output gain: about −3.1. Adm = −gm(RD∥ro), matching the large signal.

Z

Zundamon

Even with half the input each, the difference gives one common-source gain, nanoda!

7Common-mode half-circuit and gain

M

Shikoku Metan

Next, common mode. Same vic at both gates, gain measured at one output.

M

Shikoku Metan

With an ideal tail source, current can't change, the output stays put, so Acm=0.

M

Shikoku Metan

But a real tail source is episode four's mirror, with output resistance RSS.

M

Shikoku Metan

With W/L doubled and 0.2 mA flowing, RSS is about 47 kΩ.

M

Shikoku Metan

Split RSS into two parallel 2RSS. By symmetry, the wire between carries no current.

M

Shikoku Metan

Cut the wire: a common source with 2RSS at the source. The common-mode half circuit.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

What happens when the source has a resistor, nanoda?

M

Shikoku Metan

Ignoring the body, the source follows the input, so current changes by just vic/(2RSS).

M

Shikoku Metan

So Acm ≈ −RD/(2RSS). Including the body effect, it's about −0.044.

M

Shikoku Metan

Both outputs move equally, so taking their difference cancels it completely.

8Common-mode rejection ratio (CMRR)

M

Shikoku Metan

The ratio of differential to common-mode gain at the same output is the CMRR.

Shikoku Metan: At one output, differential gain is half: about 1.5. CMRR is about 35.

M

Shikoku Metan

Ignoring the body effect, CMRR ≈ gm·RSS. The tail source's output resistance sets it.

M

Shikoku Metan

To go higher, raise RSS. That's where episode six's cascode current source comes in.

M

Shikoku Metan

But the tail source uses voltage headroom, so the input DC level must be higher.

9The active-load differential pair

Z

ZundamonQuestion

How do we hand the output to the next stage on a single wire, nanoda?

M

Shikoku Metan

One side alone gives half the gain. So use episode four's PMOS mirror as the load.

M

Shikoku Metan

M3 takes M1's current and M4 copies it. The output is the drains of M2 and M4.

M

Shikoku Metan

With matched devices and equal inputs, both drains sit at about 0.97 V.

M

Shikoku Metan

When vid raises M1's current, M4 copies it and pushes current into the output.

M

Shikoku Metan

At the same time, M2 pulls less current. Both push the output up.

M

Shikoku Metan

Both sides' current changes gather into one output: single-ended conversion.

M

Shikoku Metan

The output resistance is ro2∥ro4, so the gain is Av≈gm(ro2∥ro4).

Z

Zundamon

It's the same form as episode four's active load, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. But unlike episode four, it's non-inverting. Raise VIN1 and the output rises.

M

Shikoku Metan

The gain is about +31, roughly 10 times the resistor-load difference gain.

M

Shikoku Metan

The mirror cancels common mode. Both inputs up 0.1 V moves the output only about 2 mV.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Does that also fix episode four's drifting operating point, nanoda?

M

Shikoku Metan

Tougher against common-mode drift. But input DC must stay within about 1.07 to 1.50 V.

Shikoku Metan: At the low end, the tail source loses headroom and RSS drops.

M

Shikoku Metan

It's still sensitive to the input difference. Negative feedback, later, will tame that.

10Summary

M

Shikoku Metan

To sum up: a tail source fixes the total current; the pair amplifies only the difference.

Z

Zundamon

With an active load there's one output, and the gain is gm(ro∥ro), nanoda!

Z

Zundamon

Equal noise cancels in the difference. Single-output gain goes in my report, nanoda!
