2Three ways to connect a MOSFET

M

Shikoku Metan

The input goes to the gate or the source, and the output comes from the drain or the source. Input and output can't share a terminal, so that leaves three combinations.

M

Shikoku Metan

Gate in, drain out: that's the common-source stage we've used.

Shikoku Metan: Gate in, source out: common drain, also called a source follower.

M

Shikoku Metan

Source in, drain out: that's common gate.

M

Shikoku Metan

"Common" means the remaining terminal is at ground in the small-signal circuit.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Why not put the input at the drain?

M

Shikoku Metan

In saturation, drain voltage barely changes current, making it a poor input.

3Source follower: large-signal behavior

M

Shikoku Metan

First, the source follower. Drain to VDD, with a current sink at the source.

Shikoku Metan: VDD=1.8 V and I0=0.1 mA. Episode four's mirror could provide the current.

M

Shikoku Metan

For this calculation, though, use an ideal sink with infinite output resistance.

M

Shikoku Metan

Fixed current nearly fixes VGS, so VOUT=VIN−VGS.

Z

Zundamon

On the graph, VOUT follows just below VIN!

M

Shikoku Metan

That's why it's a follower. At VIN=1.2 V, VOUT is about 0.42 V.

Shikoku Metan: DC shifts down by VGS≈0.78 V, but changes pass through almost unchanged.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Is the slope exactly one?

M

Shikoku Metan

Measuring the slope on the graph gives about 0.83, a little less than one. Let's see why with the small-signal circuit.

4Source follower: small-signal gain and body effect

M

Shikoku Metan

Step one: short the DC supply VDD and open the ideal current source. With VDD shorted, the drain is at signal ground.

M

Shikoku Metan

Step two: replace the MOSFET with its small-signal model.

M

Shikoku Metan

The gate has vin and source vout, so vgs=vin−vout.

Shikoku Metan: Step three: KCL at the output, gm(vin−vout) = vout/ro, gives Av=vout/vin=gm/(gm+1/ro), about 0.99.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

But the graph gave 0.83. What's missing?

M

Shikoku Metan

Episode two's body effect. Body at ground and source at VOUT means VSB=VOUT.

M

Shikoku Metan

As the output rises, Vth rises too, so passing the same I0 takes a larger VGS. That's why the output rises less than the input.

M

Shikoku Metan

In the small-signal model, add a second current source gmb·vbs beside gm·vgs, where vbs is the small-signal body-source voltage. The body is grounded, so vbs = −vout.

M

Shikoku Metan

gmb is body transconductance. Here gm≈0.66 mA/V and gmb is about 20% of it.

M

Shikoku Metan

Av=gm/(gm+gmb+1/ro) ≈ 0.83, matching the graph.

M

Shikoku Metan

Tying the body to the source restores about 0.99, but in an ordinary NMOS the body is the p-substrate shared by the whole chip, and it's tied to ground.

Z

Zundamon

So episode two's body effect matters here!

5Source-follower output resistance

Z

ZundamonQuestion

What use is a stage with gain below one?

M

Shikoku Metan

The key is output resistance. Apply a test voltage vx at the output and find the current ix flowing in.

M

Shikoku Metan

Set the input signal to zero. The gate and body are then at ground while the source is at vx, so vgs and vbs both equal −vx.

Shikoku Metan: Adding the two current sources and the current in ro gives ix = (gm+gmb+1/ro)vx. The current is proportional to vx, and the coefficient is the same as the gain's denominator.

Z

Zundamon

A current source behaving like a resistor!

M

Shikoku Metan

Right. The inverse gives Rout≈1.3 kΩ, including body effect.

M

Shikoku Metan

Ignoring gmb and ro gives the handy estimate Rout≈1/gm, about 1.5 kΩ.

Shikoku Metan: Much smaller than episode three's common-source output resistance of 4.6 kΩ.

6The role of a buffer

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Why is lower output resistance useful?

M

Shikoku Metan

For small signals, an amplifier's output can be modeled as a voltage source Av·vin in series with Rout: the Thévenin equivalent from circuit theory.

M

Shikoku Metan

Connect a load RL that carries only the signal and no DC (say, through a coupling capacitor), and the output is divided between Rout and RL.

M

Shikoku Metan

Episode four had gain −35 and Rout≈54 kΩ. Add RL=10 kΩ...

Z

Zundamon

−35×10/(54+10): the gain drops to about −5.5! Just like my experiment, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

Insert a source follower. Its gate draws no current, preserving the first stage's gain.

M

Shikoku Metan

Assume the DC levels between stages are adjusted, and consider only small signals.

Shikoku Metan: The previous stage's output sits at about 0.9 V DC. Connected directly, the follower output would be about 0.17 V, below the roughly 0.3 V the current sink needs to stay in saturation.

M

Shikoku Metan

The follower's Rout is about 1.3 kΩ. −35×0.83×10/(1.3+10) gives an overall gain of about −26.

M

Shikoku Metan

A stage that drives the load this way is a buffer: a key role for the follower.

7The common-gate stage

M

Shikoku Metan

Third, common gate. Use the same device and bias as episode three for comparison.

M

Shikoku Metan

Fix the gate at 0.8 V DC, apply vin at the source, and take output at the drain.

Shikoku Metan: Source DC is zero, so the bias is unchanged: VOUT≈0.92 V.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

If the source voltage rises, what happens to the output?

M

Shikoku Metan

VGS and current fall, so VOUT rises. Unlike common source, it doesn't invert.

M

Shikoku Metan

For small signals, vgs=0−vin = −vin. Ignoring body effect and ro, Av = +gm·RD.

M

Shikoku Metan

The source moves here too, so gmb comes in. vbs is −vin, the same as vgs, so this time gmb adds to gm and raises the gain.

M

Shikoku Metan

Including ro, Av = (gm+gmb+1/ro)(RD∥ro) ≈ +5.1.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Does current flow into a source input?

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. Rin≈1/(gm+gmb), about 1 kΩ: the same form as the follower's Rout.

Shikoku Metan: Its low input resistance makes it good at receiving signals supplied as currents.

M

Shikoku Metan

With an ideal voltage-source input, Rout=RD∥ro≈4.6 kΩ, like common source.

8Comparing the three basic amplifiers

M

Shikoku Metan

Let's summarize with approximate formulas, omitting ro and gmb.

M

Shikoku Metan

Gain: common source −gm·RD, common gate +gm·RD, source follower about one.

Shikoku Metan: Input resistance: infinite at the two gate inputs; about 1/gm for common gate.

Shikoku Metan: Output resistance: RD at drain outputs, about 1/gm for the source follower.

Z

Zundamon

Whenever the source is input or output, 1/gm appears!

M

Shikoku Metan

A useful rule: low resistance looking into the source, high looking into the drain.

M

Shikoku Metan

These estimates depend on what connects to the other terminals. That will be the key next time.
