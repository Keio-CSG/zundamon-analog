2Three ways to connect a MOSFET

M

Shikoku Metan

Gate or source input, drain or source output: there are three combinations.

M

Shikoku Metan

Gate in, drain out: that's the common-source stage we've used.

Shikoku Metan: Gate in, source out: common drain, also called a source follower.

M

Shikoku Metan

Source in, drain out: that's common gate.

M

Shikoku Metan

The remaining terminal connects to ground in the small-signal circuit.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Why not put the input at the drain?

M

Shikoku Metan

In saturation, drain voltage barely changes current, making it a poor input.

3Source follower: large-signal behavior

M

Shikoku Metan

First, the source follower. Drain to VDD, with a current sink at the source.

Shikoku Metan: VDD=1.8 V and I0=0.1 mA. Episode four's mirror could provide the current.

M

Shikoku Metan

For this calculation, though, use an ideal sink with infinite output resistance.

M

Shikoku Metan

Fixed current nearly fixes VGS, so VOUT=VIN−VGS.

Z

Zundamon

On the graph, VOUT follows just below VIN!

M

Shikoku Metan

That's why it's a follower. At VIN=1.2 V, VOUT is about 0.42 V.

Shikoku Metan: DC shifts down by VGS≈0.78 V, but changes pass through almost unchanged.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Is the slope exactly one?

M

Shikoku Metan

Measured here, about 0.83. Let's use the small-signal circuit to see why.

4Source follower: small-signal gain and body effect

M

Shikoku Metan

Step one: short VDD and open the current source. The drain is signal ground.

M

Shikoku Metan

Step two: replace the MOSFET with its small-signal model.

M

Shikoku Metan

The gate has vin and source vout, so vgs=vin−vout.

Shikoku Metan: Step three: KCL gives Av=gm/(gm+1/ro), about 0.99.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

But the graph gave 0.83. What's missing?

M

Shikoku Metan

Episode two's body effect. Body at ground and source at VOUT means VSB=VOUT.

M

Shikoku Metan

Rising output raises Vth and reduces current, slowing the output's rise.

M

Shikoku Metan

Add a small-signal current source gmb·vbs. Here vbs = −vout.

M

Shikoku Metan

gmb is body transconductance. Here gm≈0.66 mA/V and gmb is about 20% of it.

M

Shikoku Metan

Av=gm/(gm+gmb+1/ro) ≈ 0.83, matching the graph.

M

Shikoku Metan

Tying body to source restores about 0.99, but ordinary NMOS shares a common body.

Z

Zundamon

So episode two's body effect matters here!

5Source-follower output resistance

Z

ZundamonQuestion

What use is a stage with gain below one?

M

Shikoku Metan

Output resistance! Apply test voltage vx at the output and find incoming ix.

M

Shikoku Metan

Set the input signal to zero. Then vgs and vbs both equal −vx.

Shikoku Metan: ix = (gm+gmb+1/ro)vx, with the same coefficient as the gain denominator.

Z

Zundamon

A current source behaving like a resistor!

M

Shikoku Metan

Right. The inverse gives Rout≈1.3 kΩ, including body effect.

M

Shikoku Metan

Ignoring gmb and ro, about 1/gm≈1.5 kΩ is a handy estimate.

Shikoku Metan: Much smaller than episode three's common-source output resistance of 4.6 kΩ.

6The role of a buffer

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Why is lower output resistance useful?

M

Shikoku Metan

An amplifier's signal output is a source Av·vin in series with Rout.

M

Shikoku Metan

A signal-only load RL forms a voltage divider with Rout.

M

Shikoku Metan

Episode four had gain −35 and Rout≈54 kΩ. Add RL=10 kΩ...

Z

Zundamon

The gain drops to about −5.5! Just like my experiment!

M

Shikoku Metan

Insert a source follower. Its gate draws no current, preserving the first stage's gain.

M

Shikoku Metan

Assume the DC levels between stages are adjusted, and consider only small signals.

Shikoku Metan: Direct connection would give about 0.17 V at the follower output: too little headroom.

M

Shikoku Metan

Rout is about 1.3 kΩ. Even with follower gain 0.83, overall gain is about −26.

M

Shikoku Metan

A stage that drives the load this way is a buffer: a key role for the follower.

7The common-gate stage

M

Shikoku Metan

Third, common gate. Use the same device and bias as episode three for comparison.

M

Shikoku Metan

Fix the gate at 0.8 V DC, apply vin at the source, and take output at the drain.

Shikoku Metan: Source DC is zero, so the bias is unchanged: VOUT≈0.92 V.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

If the source voltage rises, what happens to the output?

M

Shikoku Metan

VGS and current fall, so VOUT rises. Unlike common source, it doesn't invert.

M

Shikoku Metan

vgs = −vin. Ignoring body effect and ro, Av = +gm·RD.

M

Shikoku Metan

The moving source brings in gmb. Here it adds to gm and increases the gain.

M

Shikoku Metan

Including ro, Av = (gm+gmb+1/ro)(RD∥ro) ≈ +5.1.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Does current flow into a source input?

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. Rin≈1/(gm+gmb), about 1 kΩ, similar to the follower's Rout.

Shikoku Metan: Its low input resistance makes it good at receiving signals supplied as currents.

M

Shikoku Metan

With an ideal voltage-source input, Rout=RD∥ro≈4.6 kΩ, like common source.

8Comparing the three basic amplifiers

M

Shikoku Metan

Let's summarize with approximate formulas, omitting ro and gmb.

M

Shikoku Metan

Gain: common source −gm·RD, common gate +gm·RD, source follower about one.

Shikoku Metan: Input resistance: infinite at the two gate inputs; about 1/gm for common gate.

Shikoku Metan: Output resistance: RD at drain outputs, about 1/gm for the source follower.

Z

Zundamon

Whenever the source is input or output, 1/gm appears!

M

Shikoku Metan

A useful rule: low resistance looking into the source, high looking into the drain.

M

Shikoku Metan

These estimates depend on what connects to the other terminals. That's next time's key.
