2Transistors in modern ICs: CMOS

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Please! So what does a transistor actually do, anyway?

M

Shikoku Metan

It's a device that switches current on and off, and controls how much flows.

M

Shikoku Metan

Most transistors in today's ICs, like smartphone CPUs, are MOSFETs.

M

Shikoku Metan

They're combined using a scheme called CMOS.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

"Most"? So there are other kinds of transistors too?

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes, the bipolar transistor, or BJT. Let's compare the two.

3BJT vs. MOSFET

M

Shikoku Metan

On the left is an npn BJT, on the right an N-channel MOSFET.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

They both carry current, so what's the difference?

M

Shikoku Metan

In a BJT, you forward-bias the pn junction between base and emitter with VBE.

Shikoku Metan: And the collector is held at a higher potential than the base.

Shikoku Metan: Electrons injected from the emitter mostly cross the thin base to the collector.

Shikoku Metan: Collector current IC varies exponentially with VBE. A small base current flows too.

Shikoku Metan: A MOSFET uses its gate voltage to create an electron path at the surface.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Does current flow into the gate?

M

Shikoku Metan

No. A thin insulating layer separates it, so almost no DC current flows.

Shikoku Metan: Here's a table summarizing the differences.

M

Shikoku Metan

This series focuses on the MOSFET. Today, let's see how it works.

Z

Zundamon

Let's do this, nanoda!

4MOSFET symbols and terminals

M

Shikoku Metan

Back to the N-channel MOSFET, or NMOS. Its three terminals are G, D, and S.

M

Shikoku Metan

There's a fourth, the body terminal, but let's assume it's tied to the source.

Shikoku Metan: The gate-source voltage VGS controls the current ID flowing from drain to source.

M

Shikoku Metan

This circuit applies VGS and the drain-source voltage VDS, and measures ID.

M

Shikoku Metan

Numbers here are illustrative values, assuming roughly a 180 nm CMOS process.

Z

Zundamon

A voltage that sets a current... like a dependent source from circuit theory!

M

Shikoku Metan

Good eye. Under the right conditions, it is a voltage-controlled current source.

Shikoku Metan: Let's check those conditions by varying the voltages in this circuit.

5Why current flows: the channel in cross-section

M

Shikoku Metan

Let's look at a cross-section: n-type source and drain in a p-type substrate.

Shikoku Metan: On top, a thin oxide layer sits between the substrate and the gate.

M

Shikoku Metan

The n+ regions have many electrons; the p-type substrate has many holes.

Shikoku Metan: A hole is a missing electron that moves like a positive charge.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

It's p-type between source and drain, so electrons can't pass?

M

Shikoku Metan

Right. The pn junction is an energy hill that electrons can hardly climb.

Shikoku Metan: So while VGS is small, almost no current flows. That's the cutoff region.

M

Shikoku Metan

With a positive gate voltage, holes at the surface are pushed away first.

Shikoku Metan: This region, with few mobile charges, is called the depletion layer.

M

Shikoku Metan

Raise it further: electrons gather, the hill drops, and source and drain connect.

M

Shikoku Metan

This electron path is called the channel, or the inversion layer.

Shikoku Metan: The VGS where the channel starts to form is the threshold voltage Vth.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Once there's a path, which way do the electrons flow?

M

Shikoku Metan

With VDS applied, the electric field pushes electrons from source to drain.

Shikoku Metan: Electrons are negative, so current runs opposite: ID flows from drain to source.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

If I raise VGS more, does the channel get thicker?

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. The more electrons there are, the more easily current flows.

6ID–VGS characteristics and the square law

M

Shikoku Metan

Hold VDS constant and plot ID against VGS, and you get this graph.

Z

Zundamon

It's almost zero up to Vth, then starts to rise!

M

Shikoku Metan

With large VDS and VGS well above Vth, ID is roughly proportional to (VGS−Vth)².

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Is VGS−Vth just how far we are above threshold?

M

Shikoku Metan

Right. We call it the overdrive voltage, Vov. It's a very important quantity.

7Inside the current equation: μnCox and W/L

Z

ZundamonQuestion

What are μnCox and W/L in that formula, anyway?

M

Shikoku Metan

Current is charge per unit length of channel times the average drift speed.

M

Shikoku Metan

First, the charge at small VDS. The gate and channel form an oxide capacitor.

M

Shikoku Metan

As Q = CV says, electrons collect in the channel in proportion to VGS above Vth.

M

Shikoku Metan

Cox is the oxide capacitance per unit area. Thinner oxide means larger Cox.

Shikoku Metan: Multiply by the width W, and charge per length is about Cox·W·(VGS−Vth).

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Then what decides the electrons' speed?

M

Shikoku Metan

Proportional to the field. The factor is mobility μn: how easily electrons move.

M

Shikoku Metan

By the way, holes have lower mobility than electrons. That matters for PMOS later.

M

Shikoku Metan

L is the channel length. Field ≈ VDS/L, so a shorter L means faster electrons.

M

Shikoku Metan

Multiply them together and, for small VDS, ID is this. It's proportional to VDS.

Z

Zundamon

A wider W means more electrons; a shorter L means faster ones. So ID increases!

M

Shikoku Metan

Raise VDS and the channel narrows at the drain, so charge and speed vary along it.

M

Shikoku Metan

Working it out, the current levels off and gives the first formula, with a ½.

M

Shikoku Metan

Let's write μnCox·W/L as K. In this model, K = 2 mA/V².

8The subthreshold region

Z

ZundamonQuestion

By the way, is the current really zero below Vth?

M

Shikoku Metan

I said cutoff, but it isn't really zero. Let's make the vertical axis logarithmic.

M

Shikoku Metan

Even below Vth, ID increases exponentially with VGS.

Shikoku Metan: This is called the subthreshold region, or weak inversion.

M

Shikoku Metan

In this model, ID rises tenfold for every ~90 mV increase in VGS.

Shikoku Metan: At room temperature, a normal MOSFET needs at least ~60 mV per decade of current.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

If it's not zero, is current leaking even when it's off?

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. With billions on a chip, that adds up to leakage you can't ignore.

Shikoku Metan: Conversely, some ultra-low-power circuits deliberately use this tiny current.

9ID–VDS characteristics and the linear region

M

Shikoku Metan

Back above Vth. Now let's fix VGS and vary VDS.

Z

Zundamon

It rises straight at first, then flattens out.

M

Shikoku Metan

As before, at small VDS, ID is nearly proportional to VDS, like a resistor.

Z

Zundamon

Changing VGS changes the slope. It's like a variable resistor!

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. This is called the linear region, or triode region.

10Pinch-off

Z

ZundamonQuestion

So why does it flatten out partway?

M

Shikoku Metan

As VDS rises, the gate-drain voltage VGD gets smaller at the drain end.

Shikoku Metan: So the closer to the drain, the thinner the channel gets.

Shikoku Metan: When VGD drops below Vth, the channel vanishes at the drain end.

Shikoku Metan: This is called pinch-off.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

If the channel breaks, doesn't the current stop?

M

Shikoku Metan

It doesn't. A strong field sweeps electrons across the gap in one go.

Shikoku Metan: The remaining channel largely sets the current, so the curve is nearly flat.

11Saturation and the voltage-controlled current source

M

Shikoku Metan

Back to the graph. Plot several curves with different VGS and you get this.

M

Shikoku Metan

The boundary is VDS=VGS−Vth. Linear on its left, saturation on its right.

Z

Zundamon

In saturation, ID barely changes even when VDS changes.

M

Shikoku Metan

And ID is set by VGS. That's the voltage-controlled current source from earlier.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

So amplifiers use the saturation region?

M

Shikoku Metan

Exactly. We'll cover amplifiers properly another time.

Shikoku Metan: Look closely: it slopes up slightly, right? That's channel-length modulation.

Z

Zundamon

So it's not a perfect current source.

M

Shikoku Metan

Right. We'll look at that closely next time.

12Summary: three operating regions

M

Shikoku Metan

Let's sum up. A MOSFET has three operating regions.

Z

Zundamon

Compare VGS with Vth, and VDS with VGS−Vth, and you can tell the region!

13PMOS and the CMOS inverter

Z

ZundamonQuestion

So what is CMOS, the thing you mentioned at the start?

M

Shikoku Metan

To explain, let me introduce another MOSFET: the P-channel PMOS.

M

Shikoku Metan

In CMOS, PMOS sits in an n-well in the p-substrate; n and p reversed from NMOS.

Shikoku Metan: With the gate well below the source, surface holes gather to form a path.

Shikoku Metan: At equal W/L and voltage magnitudes, PMOS carries less: holes move less easily.

Shikoku Metan: Its symbol has a circle on the gate. The source is usually drawn on top.

M

Shikoku Metan

PMOS on top, NMOS below: top source to supply VDD, bottom source to ground.

M

Shikoku Metan

Tie the gates together as the input, and the drains together as the output.

Shikoku Metan: With 0 V in, the PMOS is on and the NMOS is off. The output connects to VDD.

M

Shikoku Metan

Input at VDD is the reverse: the NMOS turns on and the output goes to 0 V.

Z

Zundamon

The output is always the opposite of the input!

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. This is the inverter, the NOT gate of digital logic.

M

Shikoku Metan

With VDD=1.8 V, sweep the input from 0 to VDD. The output changes like this.

M

Shikoku Metan

Here, the output switches sharply when the input nears 0.9 V, half of VDD.

M

Shikoku Metan

For that, this example makes the PMOS W/L twice the NMOS's, so the currents match.

Shikoku Metan: NMOS and PMOS work as complements: hence Complementary MOS, or CMOS.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

What's good about one of them being off?

M

Shikoku Metan

With the input held at 0 or VDD, one device is off, so almost no current flows.

Shikoku Metan: So power consumption is low. That's why nearly all of today's ICs are CMOS.

Z

Zundamon

So that's what "today's ICs are CMOS" meant at the start. I finally get it!
