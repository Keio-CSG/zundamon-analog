2Review: the intrinsic-gain limit

M

Shikoku Metan

Recap: episode four's active-load gain was Av = −gm1(ro1∥ro2).

Shikoku Metan: Gain is gm times output resistance. It came to about −35, half the intrinsic gain.

M

Shikoku Metan

Today we raise Rout by circuit design. The key: last time's common gate.

M

Shikoku Metan

Seen from the source, about 1/gm. Seen from the drain, large. We'll use that.

3The cascode circuit

M

Shikoku Metan

Stack a common-gate M2 on top of the common-source M1. That's the cascode.

Shikoku Metan: M2's gate is fixed at a constant voltage Vb. Call the node between them X.

M

Shikoku Metan

In series, the current is the same. M1 turns input into current; M2 passes it up.

M

Shikoku Metan

Last time's common gate accepts a current input. That's useful here.

M

Shikoku Metan

VDD=1.8 V. M1's gate is about 0.70 V like episode four's mirror, and Vb=1.25 V.

Shikoku Metan: M2's body is grounded and its source is X, so we include the body effect.

4Why output resistance increases

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Why does stacking alone make the output resistance bigger?

M

Shikoku Metan

Watch the voltage at X, VX. M1's VDS is exactly VX.

M

Shikoku Metan

Raise the output from 1.0 V to 1.5 V, and VX moves only about 6 mV.

Z

Zundamon

From M1's view, VDS hardly changes, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

So M1's current barely changes either. M2 shields M1 from the output.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

But M2's VDS changes a lot, nanoda. Doesn't M2's current increase?

M

Shikoku Metan

A small rise in VX lowers M2's VGS, which cancels the extra current.

M

Shikoku Metan

Current rises about 4.5% for a single MOSFET, but only about 0.07% for the cascode.

5Deriving output resistance with a small-signal model

M

Shikoku Metan

Let's find Rout with equations. Attach a test source vt to the output.

M

Shikoku Metan

Call the incoming current it. The input is zero, and Vb is ground for small signals.

M

Shikoku Metan

M1 has vgs1=0, so its current source vanishes, leaving only ro1.

M

Shikoku Metan

All of that current flows through M2 into ro1, so vx = it·ro1.

M

Shikoku Metan

M2's gate and body are grounded, so vgs2 and vbs2 both equal −vx.

M

Shikoku Metan

So its current source tries to push (gm2+gmb2)vx upward.

Z

Zundamon

Upward, nanoda? The test current flows downward, nanoda.

M

Shikoku Metan

Right. So ro2 carries the test current plus the source's, all downward.

M

Shikoku Metan

Rout is ro1+ro2, plus (gm2+gmb2)ro2·ro1.

M

Shikoku Metan

The last term is far larger, so Rout ≈ (gm2+gmb2)ro2·ro1.

Shikoku Metan: Ignoring the body, ro1 is multiplied by the intrinsic gain gm2·ro2.

Z

Zundamon

What the source connects to changes so much, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. Last time the source was grounded; now M2's source connects to ro1.

Shikoku Metan: At VOUT=1.2 V, Rout is about 7.1 MΩ: 66 times the single device's 107 kΩ.

6Comparing output current–voltage curves

M

Shikoku Metan

Plot VOUT on the horizontal axis and compare the single device with the cascode.

Z

Zundamon

The cascode is much flatter, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

The inverse slope is Rout. The graph also gives about 7.1 MΩ, matching the formula.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

But the cascode's current is a bit smaller, nanoda?

M

Shikoku Metan

M1's VDS stops at VX. It's the single device's current at VDS=VX.

Shikoku Metan: Also, it starts flattening further right than the single device. That matters later.

7The cascode current mirror

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Can this shrink episode four's current-mirror error too, nanoda?

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. Episode four's mirror carried about 7% extra current at VDS=1.5 V.

M

Shikoku Metan

Stack two diode-connected devices on the input too. Then VX ≈ the left VGS.

M

Shikoku Metan

In this matched model, even at 1.5 V output the error is under 0.01%.

Shikoku Metan: This is called a cascode current mirror.

M

Shikoku Metan

But Vb is about 1.55 V. Output must be above about 1.0 V to flatten.

8The cascode amplifier

M

Shikoku Metan

Let's use it in an amplifier. Make episode four's active load cascode on both sides.

Shikoku Metan: NMOS M1 and M2 below, PMOS M3 and M4 above. The input is M1's gate.

M

Shikoku Metan

Doubling the PMOS W/L matches its Vov to the NMOS, about 0.3 V.

Shikoku Metan: PMOS bodies connect to their own sources, so there's no body effect.

M

Shikoku Metan

Gain is −gm1 times output resistance: the lower and upper cascodes in parallel.

M

Shikoku Metan

With matched gm and ro, no body effect, each is about gm·ro·ro. In parallel, half.

Shikoku Metan: So Av ≈ −(gm·ro)²/2.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

The intrinsic gain squared, nanoda! If gm·ro≈70, that's about 2500, nanoda?

M

Shikoku Metan

Computed right near the operating point, about −1400. It matches the graph's slope.

M

Shikoku Metan

Below the estimate because each VDS is small, lowering ro. More on headroom soon.

Z

Zundamon

About 40 times episode four's −35, nanoda! Just two more transistors, nanoda.

9The cost: voltage headroom

M

Shikoku Metan

But it's not all good. The price is voltage headroom.

Shikoku Metan: Saturation needs NMOS VDS and PMOS VSD each at least Vov. Four of them: about 1.2 V.

Z

Zundamon

The supply is 1.8 V, so the output can only move about 0.6 V, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

With margin for M1 and M4 too, all stay saturated only for about 0.76 to 1.05 V.

M

Shikoku Metan

Episode four's circuit ran about 0.32 to 1.37 V, a span of 1 V. Now it's about 0.3 V.

Shikoku Metan: Referred to the input, that span is only about 0.34 mV. Input DC must be precise.

M

Shikoku Metan

A small VDS also lowers ro. That's why we fell short of the estimate of about 2500.

M

Shikoku Metan

Advanced processes have low intrinsic gain and low supplies. Stacking is hard.

10Summary

M

Shikoku Metan

To sum up: stacking a common gate on a common source is the cascode.

Shikoku Metan: M2 shields M1, raising the output resistance to about gm·ro·ro.

Z

Zundamon

Mirrors and amplifiers both improve, but we lose voltage headroom, nanoda!

Zundamon: In the assignment, I'll check headroom as well as gain, nanoda!

Shikoku Metan
