Cascodes: Raising Output Resistance

Zundamon

My design assignment's in trouble again, nanoda! I want more gain without changing the 1.8 V supply.

Z

Zundamon

Let's do it, nanoda! This time we solve the assignment!

2Review: the intrinsic-gain limit

M

Shikoku Metan

Recap: episode four's active-load gain was Av = −gm1(ro1∥ro2).

Shikoku Metan: Gain is gm times output resistance. It came to about −35, half the intrinsic gain gm·ro of about 70.

M

Shikoku Metan

Today we raise Rout by circuit design. The key: last time's common gate.

M

Shikoku Metan

Looking into the source, the resistance is about 1/gm; looking into the drain, it's large. We'll use that difference.

3The cascode circuit

M

Shikoku Metan

Stack a common-gate M2 on top of the common-source M1. That's the cascode.

M

Shikoku Metan

M2's gate is fixed at a constant voltage Vb. Call the node between them X.

Shikoku Metan: In series, the current is the same. M1 turns input into current; M2 passes it up.

M

Shikoku Metan

Last time I said the common-gate stage is good at receiving signals that arrive as currents. That's exactly what we use here.

M

Shikoku Metan

VDD=1.8 V. M1's gate is about 0.70 V like episode four's mirror, and Vb=1.25 V.

M

Shikoku Metan

M2's body is grounded and its source is X, so we include the body effect.

4Why output resistance increases

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Why does stacking alone make the output resistance bigger?

M

Shikoku Metan

Look at the voltage at X, VX. M1's VDS is exactly VX.

M

Shikoku Metan

Raise the output from 1.0 V to 1.5 V, and VX, about 0.45 V, moves by only about 6 mV.

Z

Zundamon

From M1's view, VDS hardly changes, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

So M1's current barely changes either. M2 shields M1 from changes in the output voltage.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

But M2's VDS changes a lot, nanoda. Doesn't M2's current increase?

M

Shikoku Metan

VX only has to rise slightly: that lowers M2's VGS=Vb−VX enough to cancel the extra current.

M

Shikoku Metan

For that same 1.0 V to 1.5 V step, the current rises about 4.5% for a single MOSFET alone (M1 by itself, gate at 0.70 V), but only about 0.07% for the cascode.

5Deriving output resistance with a small-signal model

M

Shikoku Metan

Let's find Rout with equations. Attach a test source vt to the output.

M

Shikoku Metan

Call the incoming current it. The input is zero, and Vb is ground for small signals.

M

Shikoku Metan

M1 has vgs1=0, so its current source vanishes, leaving only ro1.

M

Shikoku Metan

All of that current flows through M2 into ro1, so vx=it·ro1.

M

Shikoku Metan

M2's gate and body are grounded, so vgs2 and vbs2 both equal −vx.

M

Shikoku Metan

Then M2's current source, gm2·vgs2+gmb2·vbs2, equals −(gm2+gmb2)vx: it tries to push (gm2+gmb2)vx upward.

Z

Zundamon

Upward, nanoda? The test current flows downward, nanoda.

M

Shikoku Metan

Right. So ro2 carries the test current plus the current source's share, both flowing downward.

Shikoku Metan: Put vx=it·ro1 into vt=vx+ro2[it + (gm2+gmb2)vx], and Rout=vt/it=ro1+ro2 + (gm2+gmb2)ro2·ro1.

M

Shikoku Metan

The last term is far larger, so Rout ≈ (gm2+gmb2)ro2·ro1.

Shikoku Metan: Ignoring the body effect, this is ro1 multiplied by M2's intrinsic gain gm2·ro2, about 70.

Z

Zundamon

What the source connects to changes so much, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. Last time the source was grounded; now M2's source connects to ro1.

Shikoku Metan: At VOUT=1.2 V, Rout is about 7.1 MΩ: 66 times the single device's 107 kΩ.

6Comparing output current–voltage curves

M

Shikoku Metan

Plot VOUT on the horizontal axis and compare the single device with the cascode.

Z

Zundamon

The cascode is much flatter, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

Rout is the inverse of the slope. Read off the graph, it's also about 7.1 MΩ, matching the formula.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

But the cascode's current is a bit smaller, nanoda?

M

Shikoku Metan

Because M1's VDS stops at VX, about 0.45 V. It's the single device's current at VDS=0.45 V on its curve.

Shikoku Metan: Also, the curve flattens out only from about 0.75 V, to the right of the single device's 0.3 V. That will matter later.

7The cascode current mirror

Z

ZundamonQuestion

Can this shrink episode four's current-mirror error too, nanoda?

M

Shikoku Metan

Yes. Episode four's mirror carried about 7% extra current at VDS=1.5 V.

Shikoku Metan: Stack two diode-connected devices on the input side too, and use the upper gate as Vb. Then VX on the output side nearly equals the lower VGS on the input side, so the two lower devices have the same VDS.

M

Shikoku Metan

In this matched model, even at 1.5 V output the error is under 0.01%.

Shikoku Metan: This is called a cascode current mirror.

M

Shikoku Metan

But the upper gate voltage Vb is two VGS's stacked, about 1.55 V, and the output must stay above about 1.0 V for the curve to be flat.

8The cascode amplifier

M

Shikoku Metan

Let's use it in an amplifier too: make both the bottom and the top of episode four's active-load stage into cascodes.

Shikoku Metan: NMOS M1 and M2 below, PMOS M3 and M4 above. The input is M1's gate.

M

Shikoku Metan

Doubling the PMOS W/L matches its Vov to the NMOS, about 0.3 V.

M

Shikoku Metan

PMOS bodies connect to their own sources, so there's no body effect.

Shikoku Metan: Gain is −gm1 times output resistance: the lower and upper cascodes in parallel.

M

Shikoku Metan

If gm and ro match and we ignore body effect, each is about gm·ro·ro; in parallel, half that.

Shikoku Metan: So Av ≈ −(gm·ro)²/2.

Z

ZundamonQuestion

The intrinsic gain squared, nanoda! If gm·ro≈70, that's about 2500, nanoda?

M

Shikoku Metan

Computed right near the operating point, about −1400. It matches the graph's slope.

M

Shikoku Metan

It falls short of the estimate because each VDS is small, which lowers ro. We'll come back to that with headroom.

Z

Zundamon

About 40 times episode four's −35, nanoda! Just two more transistors, nanoda.

9The cost: voltage headroom

M

Shikoku Metan

But it's not all good. The price is voltage headroom.

Shikoku Metan: Saturation needs NMOS VDS and PMOS VSD each at least Vov. Four of them: about 1.2 V.

Z

Zundamon

The supply is 1.8 V, so the output can only move about 0.6 V, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan

With margin left for M1 and M4 in this bias, every device stays saturated only for outputs of about 0.76 to 1.05 V (M2 sets the lower limit, M3 the upper).

M

Shikoku Metan

Episode four's circuit ran about 0.32 to 1.37 V, a span of 1 V. Now it's about 0.3 V.

Shikoku Metan: With a gain of about 1400, that span is only about 0.34 mV at the input. The input DC level must be set precisely too.

M

Shikoku Metan

A small VDS also lowers ro. That's why we fell short of the estimate of about 2500.

M

Shikoku Metan

Advanced processes, with their low intrinsic gain, also run on low supply voltages, so stacking is hard. That's the dilemma.

10Summary

M

Shikoku Metan

To sum up: stacking a common gate on a common source is the cascode.

Shikoku Metan: M2 shields M1, raising the output resistance to about gm·ro·ro.

Z

Zundamon

Mirrors and amplifiers both improve, but we lose voltage headroom, nanoda!

Zundamon: In the assignment, I'll check headroom as well as gain, nanoda!

M

Shikoku Metan
